Ultrasound of the whole abdomen was performed as part of the annual health check-up, after an overnight fast, using a 3.5 MHz curvilinear transducer.

The liver is normal in size, spanning about 14.1 cm, with a mild diffuse rise in parenchymal echogenicity; the portal vein walls and the diaphragm remain clearly seen, suggesting mild fatty change of the liver. There is no focal lesion and the intrahepatic biliary channels are of normal calibre. The gall bladder is adequately distended and contains multiple mobile echogenic calculi casting posterior acoustic shadows, the largest measuring approximately 1.4 cm, along with a thin layer of dependent sludge. Its wall is not thickened at 2.5 mm and there is no fluid around it. The common bile duct measures 4.2 mm and is not dilated; no calculus is seen within its visualised portion.

The pancreas is seen well up to the tail and shows normal size and echotexture, without any ductal dilatation. The spleen measures 9.8 cm and appears normal. Both kidneys are normal in size and position with well maintained corticomedullary differentiation, and neither shows a calculus or hydronephrosis. The urinary bladder is well distended with a thin wall and clear contents, and the prostate, at about 18 cc, is of normal size with homogeneous echoes. There is no ascites.

In summary, the study shows cholelithiasis with sludge and grade I fatty liver. Surgical opinion may be considered for the gall bladder calculi.
